# MainStreet AI: Research Notes

Prepared 2026-11-08. Every figure carries a source link and the year it describes. Figures from vendors are labelled as vendor data. Sources are listed with dates at the end.

## 1. Who small businesses are

Nationally, the SBA Office of Advocacy reports 36,207,130 small businesses as of February 2026. They are 99.9% of businesses, employ 62.3 million people (45.9% of private-sector workers) and account for 43.5% of GDP [S1].

In Maryland, the 2025 Advocacy state profile (built on Census Statistics of U.S. Businesses data for 2022) lists 696,710 small businesses, 99.6% of Maryland businesses, employing 1.2 million people, 47.9% of Maryland employees [S2]. This is the figure for the 2025 profile; the Maryland employment share was 48.8% in the 2023 profile [S3].

## 2. AI adoption and barriers

Two surveys give very different numbers because they ask different questions and sample differently.

The Census Bureau's Business Trends and Outlook Survey (BTOS) asks whether a business used AI in any business function in the past two weeks. From December 2025 to May 2026, overall use stayed between 17% and 20%, and was 19.8% as of May 3, 2026. Use did not change significantly among firms with fewer than 20 employees, and fewer than 20% of firms with four or fewer employees used AI. Retail trade was about 14% [S4].

The U.S. Chamber of Commerce's 2025 survey (3,870 small businesses with fewer than 250 employees, fielded June 2025) found 58% say they use generative AI, up from 40% in 2024 and 23% in 2023. In Maryland the figure was 56%, and 86% of Maryland respondents believed AI will help their business in future [S5][S6]. The Chamber number is self-identified use among a broader group; the Census number is the stricter, nationally representative one. We use both and say which is which.

Barriers: the Census AI supplement (reference period November 2025 to January 2026) asked firms not planning to use AI why. 65% said AI is not applicable to their business, 22% cited lack of knowledge of what AI can do, and 20% cited privacy or security concerns. The least common reasons were needing vendors or consultants and legal restrictions [S7]. The same questionnaire lists "too expensive" as an option, but the paper text we could read does not give its share, so we make no cost claim.

Reading of the data: most non-adopting owners do not say it is too hard. They say it does not apply to them, or they do not know what it can do. That is a discovery problem, which is what MainStreet AI addresses.

## 3. Pains by business type

**Missed calls (home services and trades).** Invoca, a call-analytics vendor, reported in May 2024 that 27% of calls to home services businesses are not answered, and that 62% of customers call before buying [S8]. Its 2026 benchmark report (70 million calls, all industries) found 56% of calls to businesses are answered by a person [S9]. For value per missed call, Housecall Pro cites Invoca research at about $1,200 per missed call for home services, a secondary citation we could not trace to the original table [S10]. Treat this as vendor data.

**Reviews and local choice (all types).** BrightLocal's Local Consumer Review Survey 2026 (published Feb 11, 2026) is the main source. Its guide to responding to reviews, updated October 2026, states that 96% of consumers read reviews for local businesses, that whether a business responds is an important factor for 46%, and that consumers are twice as likely to use a business that responds to all reviews than one that responds to none [S11]. A BrightLocal page from October 21, 2026 reports 89% of consumers expect owners to respond, and that 47% of small businesses have fewer than 20 reviews [S12]. BrightLocal also states that 31% of consumers only use businesses rated 4.5 stars or more [S13].

**Salons and booking.** The only salon figure we could source is from Zenoti, a salon software vendor: salons see an 8% cancellation rate and a 3% no-show rate (October 2025) [S14]. Other sites quote 15% to 25%, but we could not trace those to a primary source, so we leave them out. The range shows no-show claims depend heavily on who measures them.

**Restaurants.** The National Restaurant Association's 2026 State of the Restaurant Industry report (press release Feb 12, 2026) says 42% of operators reported their restaurant was not profitable in 2025, 60% reported softer customer traffic, and more than 9 in 10 cite food, labor, insurance, energy and swipe fees as significant challenges [S15]. We found no sourced figure for time spent on admin or marketing, so we make no claim about it.

## 4. How owners find tools today, and the gaps

| Directory | What its own pages say it offers | Fit for a Main Street owner |
|---|---|---|
| G2 | Business software reviews; its homepage pitches "Selling software? Reach more buyers" and G2 Marketing Solutions to vendors [S16] | Built around software buyers and sellers; no setup help |
| Capterra | Software comparison; vendors pay for sponsored profiles to receive traffic and sales opportunities, and run pay-per-click campaigns [S17][S18] | Paid placement mixes with ranking; software-category framing |
| There's An AI For That | Index of 50,000+ AI tools mapped to 11,000+ tasks. Makers pay for launch listings, sponsorships and pay-per-click; staff review each submission [S19] | Task search is useful, but listings are paid and nobody follows up after the click |
| Product Hunt | Daily curation of new products; the team page lists a Head of Ad Sales [S20] | Ranks by launch day for tech audiences, not by fit for a plumber or salon |

The gaps are structural. These sites are built for people who buy software or launch products. None promises to set a tool up for the owner, and none is accountable to a local community. Paid placement is part of each business model, which is a conflict of interest when the buyer cannot judge quality. This is our reading of their published pages, not a claim any of them makes.

## 5. Tool risk

Relay.app, an AI workflow-automation tool launched in 2021, shut down. TechCrunch (Aug 17, 2026) reports free customers lost access on August 15, 2026 and paying customers on September 14, 2026, with the closure first announced in July and the founder joining Google's Chrome team [S21]. Relay's own site now says all accounts, workflows and run history have been permanently deleted [S22]. Customers who built client workflows on it had to rebuild them; a Zapier guide to exporting workflows was published July 20, 2026 [S23].

Pricing also shifts. WIRED (Jul 9, 2026) reported Claude subscribers must soon pay usage-based fees to access Anthropic's best consumer model, describing it as a sign that the era of flat AI subscriptions is ending [S24]. We read only the search summary of that article, so we cite it for the headline claim only.

Why vetting matters: a small business that wires a tool into booking, billing or phones has no IT team to migrate it on short notice. Relay gave about one month of notice for free users and two months for paid users. A trust score should track company stability, data export, and pricing history.
